# Plan — the lens, rebuilt for one building — 2026-08-19

Written against a **measurement of the running build**, not against §7. Reproduce it with:

```bash
npm run dev                       # :5176
node scripts/screenshot.mjs "http://localhost:5176/?scenario=floor&z=0.2222" shot.png 997 448
# window.__stage carries z, rung, view, scale, tierPx, weights
```

Frame is `997x448` throughout — the largest in §23.4.2's box, and the one §13.7.1a was
measured at.

The brief this answers, in the player's words: *"the game only looks good under 100 people,
anything above is a disaster."* Four non-negotiables came with it, and they are the acceptance
criteria for everything below:

1. **Choose and zoom to desk level, and interact with any developer, at every level** —
   planet, state, city, campus, building, floor.
2. **The transition in and out is smooth**, and the room is **exactly the size of the 5x2
   grid of hundred-developer squads standing on it** — not larger.
3. **Sensible art for floors and buildings.**
4. **Click to choose which floor to go into.**

Scope for this plan: **1 to 10,000 developers, one building.** Everything above it is
deliberately out of frame and is designed for by leaving the machinery general, not by
building it.

## 1. What was measured

`window.__stage` at each scenario and each ladder stop. `tierPx` is what the dominant view
actually measures on screen; the last column is the fraction of the 997 px frame covered by
**the developers**, computed from the seat block's own bounds (see §1.1).

| Scenario | z | rung | view | scale | tierPx | people on screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 devs | 0.222 | 2 | room | 0.317 | 649x330 | **283 px — 28% of frame width** |
| 1,000 | 0.222 | 2 | room | 0.146 | 649x326 | **483 px — 48%** |
| 10,000 | 0.222 | 2 | room | 0.146 | 649x326 | 48% (only 1,000 of them drawn) |
| 10,000 | 0.333 | 3 | tower | 0.670 | 190x394 | **190 px — 19%**, no people at all |
| 10,000 | 0.444 | 4 | block | 1.701 | 229x394 | **23%**, one building, on black |

`panLimit` is `0,0` at **every one of those stops**. There is nowhere a drag can go.

### 1.1 The seat block's true size, and the three multipliers that shrink it

A squad is 10x10 seats; a floor is 5x2 squads at `SQUAD_STRIDE_COLS/ROWS` of 12.6 / 12.0.
Through `isoAt`, in room-local px:

| Block | Screen bounds | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| One squad (100) | 893 x 446 | 2:1 |
| A full floor (1,000) | **3312 x 1656** | 2:1 |

The floor's seat block is already exactly 2:1, which is the shape the landscape decision
(§23.4) exists for. Three multipliers then take it apart, in order:

| Step | 997 px becomes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `viewportForView('room')` reserves two rails | 738 px | `min(176, w*0.13)` each side |
| `FIT_MARGIN` 0.88 | 649 px | §23.4.2's HUD margin |
| the shell's surround around the seats | **483 px** | `floorBox`'s `pad = SQUAD_COLS * 0.45` |

**The third one is the defect.** `pad` is *half a squad on every side*. At ten squads that is a
10% surround and reads as a room. At **one** squad it is larger than the squad, so the shell
comes out 2046 px wide around an 893 px block — the room is **2.3x the people standing in
it**, which is the clump-in-an-empty-diamond in the 100-developer screenshot. A constant
expressed as a fraction of the child is a constant that stops being one.

### 1.2 The cross-fade partner is always ~3x off-scale, so the overlap is a fog

`ladderShrink` is `sqrt(seats(stop) / seats(z))`, so a view one rung from its own stop is
drawn at **3.16x or 1/3.16x** its fitted size. Worked through at 10,000 developers:

| Camera at | You see | And also |
|---|---|---|
| rung 2 (room) | the room, 649 px | the **tower at 1831 px**, alpha 0.094 |
| rung 3 (tower) | the tower, 190 px | the **same building at ~724 px**, alpha 0.044 |
| rung 4 (block) | the block, 229 px | the tower at ~600 px, alpha 0.094 |

At rung 3 the screen holds **two copies of one building at different sizes**, and the larger
one is the faint hexagon visible behind the tower in `building-z0.3333.png`. §10.5 asks that
nothing cut; what was built instead is a dissolve between two pictures that are never the same
size, which is the one thing that can never resolve into a camera move. **No amount of tuning
the fade fixes this, because the fade was never the problem** — the two pictures are framed
independently and then blended.

### 1.3 The numeral layer is the loudest object in the frame

`tallies.ts` `MAX_SOURCES = 100`, `TALLY_PERIOD = 1.15 s`, `TALLY_LIFE_MS = 1250` — so **about
110 numerals are in flight at all times**, at every headcount from 100 upward, at every zoom,
each independent of the camera scale. At 100 developers there is one per head. At 1,000 the
thinning kicks in and there are still 100. In both screenshots the floor is illegible under
cyan text before anything else is wrong with it.

### 1.4 Nothing above the room has a ground, a context or a person in it

Rung 3 is one pale column on black. Rung 4 at 10,000 developers is one building on black at
23% of the frame. Neither has a horizon, a plot, a neighbour, a shadow or a scale cue. The
§7.7.1 rule — *the unit changes, the studio does not recede* — is honoured in the arithmetic
and contradicted by the picture, because a unit alone in a black frame reads as smaller, not
as larger.

### 1.5 Choosing a floor exists and cannot be found

`storeyAtLocal` picks a storey, `setFocusStorey` lights it, `descendOneRung` moves the address
into it. All built, all correct. But the affordance is a **double-tap**, the *first* tap of
which pokes, there is no cursor, no label, no list and no button — and the target is a 190 px
tower divided into ten 39 px bands. The mechanism is there; nothing on screen says so.

## 2. The one change everything else follows from

> Replace **N independently-framed views cross-faded by rung distance** with **one world, one
> camera, and level-of-detail chosen by a cell's size on screen.**

Four rules:

1. **A child is authored inside its parent's cell.** A floor plate in the building view *is*
   the room, at a tenth of the size — not a second picture of it. A squad plate in the room
   *is* the 10x10 desk block. The nesting is exact and is asserted by a test:
   `cellRect(building, floor 7)` scaled by the descent transform equals the floor's own frame.
2. **One camera**: a scale and a centre, in world units. Descending into a cell is the affine
   transform that takes that cell's rect to the frame; it is interpolated in log-scale, so it
   is one continuous move. There is no per-view fit and no per-view alpha.
3. **LOD switches on a cell's on-screen size, never on camera Z.** A squad plate becomes real
   desks when it passes ~180 px wide; a desk becomes a person and a monitor at ~18 px. The two
   representations are chosen to be near-identical *at the swap size*, so the swap is
   invisible. This is what makes the zoom continuous, and it is the standard trick — the
   cross-fade was an attempt to buy the same thing with alpha, which cannot work when the two
   sides are 3.16x apart (§1.2).
4. **Only what is on screen is resolved, up to a budget.** At most ~4 squads (400 people) hold
   real bodies at once. A cell off-frame is never built. This is what makes 10,000 cost the
   same as 100 — and it is the same mechanism that will make 10^8 cost the same as 10,000, so
   it is not scaffolding.

**Smoothness stops being a tuning problem and becomes a property of the geometry**, which is
the same argument `grid.ts` already makes for the lattice and which was never carried into the
camera.

## 3. The world, for one building

Four levels. Every one of them holds ten of the next, which is why one piece of machinery
serves all four and will serve the six above them.

| Level | Unit | Holds | Frame when it is the subject | Art |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L0** | a desk | one developer | the person, their desk, their monitor | **keep what exists** — this is the part that looks good |
| **L1** | a squad | 10x10 desks | 893x446 seat block | existing desk bank + pod dressing |
| **L2** | a floor | 5x2 squads = **1,000** | **3312x1656 seat block** | room shell, corridors, lift lobby, facade |
| **L3** | a building | 10 floors = **10,000** | exploded stack of ten floor plates | **new** |

Above L3, unbuilt and unblocked: campus (10 buildings), city (10 campuses), state, country,
planet. Each is one layout function and one cell-art function; no new camera work.

### 3.1 The floor is a fixed 1,000 seats. It does not grow — it fills.

This is the direct answer to non-negotiable 2, and it is worth more than its size.

Today the shell is re-solved on every hire (`floorBox(ceil(n/100))`), so the room's extent
moves, so the camera's fit moves, so **hiring re-frames the picture**. That is a shove the
player did not ask for, and it is a second reason the zoom never settles.

A floor is 5x2 squad plates **from the moment the garage is outgrown**, whatever the
headcount. Empty squads are drawn dark and unpopulated. Consequences:

- the room is *exactly* the size of the 5x2 grid, permanently and by construction;
- the camera fit is a constant at L2, so hiring never moves the frame;
- hiring reads as **the floor filling up**, which is §6's thesis in set dressing;
- at 1,000 the building gains a storey rather than the room gaining width — growth goes
  **up**, which is what makes the building level mean anything.

The garage (1 to ~10) stays as it is: it is the founding myth and it is good. One transition,
once, at the unfold.

### 3.2 The surround is a corridor, not a fraction

Replace `pad = SQUAD_COLS * 0.45` with a **constant perimeter aisle** of 3 tiles. Measured:

| | Seat block | Plus aisle | Fit at 997x448 | People on screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| now | 3312 x 1656 | 4454 x 2238 | 0.146 | 483 px — 48% |
| proposed | 3312 x 1656 | **3907 x 1954** | 0.216 | **714 px — 72%** |

### 3.3 Fit the content, not the container

`viewportForView` reserves 130 px of rail on each side and costs the picture 26% of the frame
before anything is drawn. The room's left and right extremes are its **corridor**, not its
people. So: **fit the seat block to the safe area, and let the plate and the aisle bleed under
the rails.** Frame what matters; let scenery run off.

## 4. Art, per level

The rule: **each level has its own silhouette and its own value key**, so a glance answers
"where am I" without reading a word. Entropy tint rides over all four unchanged.

**L3 — the building.** An **exploded isometric stack** of ten floor plates, a lift core
threading them, floor numbers on the near edge of each plate, occupancy shown by how full each
plate's desk-dot texture is, and the focused plate lit and lifted slightly out of the stack.
Not the current solid slab tower: a closed box says nothing about being enterable, and its
storeys cannot be zoomed into geometrically because they are drawn edge-on. A stack of plates
*is* ten floor plans, so descending into one is a literal geometric zoom (§2 rule 1) and
"click a floor" needs no explanation. The building also gets a ground: a plot, a contact
shadow, a street edge, so it is standing somewhere rather than floating.

**L2 — the floor.** It has to read as *inside the box you just looked at*. Add: the outer wall
with windows on the two far sides, a **lift lobby at the north corner** — the thing you
arrived through, and the anchor that makes ascending legible — corridor bands between the
plates (already modelled, not yet dressed), a floor-number decal on the slab, and a ceiling
edge so the room has a lid. Existing props (§7.8.1's table) stay and thin out with crowding as
designed.

**L1 — the squad.** A pod, not a plate: the desk banks it already has, plus a whiteboard, a
plant, a bin, and a squad number stencilled on the floor. This is the level where §4.9a's
spread between people is meant to be visible, so it wants to be worth looking at.

**L0 — the desk.** Unchanged. It is the reference the other three are being brought up to.

**Value key**, coolest and darkest at the top: building exterior — floor — squad — desk, each
a step warmer and brighter. Zooming in should feel like going indoors.

## 5. Navigation, and the floor picker

- **Tap a cell = select it.** A cursor lands on it, and a label names it:
  `FLOOR 07 · 1,000 DEVS · 12% ENTROPY`. Selecting is free and costs no Entropy.
- **Tap the selected cell again, or press ENTER, = descend into it.** Two taps on the same
  thing, no timing window, no double-tap, no race with the poke.
- **A lift panel** at L3: ten buttons, 10 down to 1, with occupancy bars. At L2 the same
  control is a 5x2 mini-map of squads. This is non-negotiable 4, answered directly, and it is
  also how a player reaches a floor that is off screen.
- **A breadcrumb**, always on: `BUILDING 1 / FLOOR 07 / SQUAD 3 / DESK 41`, every segment
  tappable to ascend to it. This is the cheapest complete answer to non-negotiable 1 — every
  level is one tap away, at every moment, and the current level is never in doubt.
- **Pinch and wheel keep working**, with **magnetic stops**: the camera eases to the nearest
  level when the gesture ends, so it never parks between two levels. The address
  (`focusSeat`, which is already the right design) decides *which* cell it settles into.
- **Poke** stays on the selected cell, at every level, as §4.5b already has it.

## 8. Cost, stated plainly

- **`room.ts` is 3,366 lines** and mixes layout, art, animation and state. Stage 4 should split
  it: `floorLayout.ts` (pure), `deskArt.ts`, `roomShell.ts`, `roomHandle.ts`. Not doing this
  makes stages 4 and 6 much harder than they need to be.
- **`npm run check` will go red across several gates.** `test:ui-frame` (68 screens) and
  `test:walk` (~15 minutes) both encode the current ladder. The walk's navigation legs have to
  be rewritten against the address model in stage 8; budget for it rather than discovering it.
- **This is a GDD change, not only a code change.** §7.4, §7.4a, §7.7.1's rung table and §20.2's
  audio zone edges are all written in terms of tiers and bands that stage 3 deletes. The
  convention here is that a finding gets a date and a file; §7 needs a dated rewrite alongside
  stage 3, keeping the original text visible.

## 9. Decisions this plan did not make — answered 2026-08-19/20

1. **The building's register** — exploded plate stack (recommended, and assumed above) versus a
   cutaway facade. The facade looks more like a building and cannot be zoomed into
   geometrically; the stack can.
2. **The fixed floor** — 1,000 permanent seats that fill (recommended) versus a room that keeps
   growing. The second keeps §7.8.1's "walls push outward" line literally true and costs a
   stable camera.
3. **How far to go now** — the four levels above L3 are designed for and not built. If the
   answer is "build campus too", stage 5 gets a sibling and nothing else changes.

**Answered.** All three took the recommended option:

1. **The building's register** — the exploded plate stack. It then failed twice by eye and was
   replaced; see `HANDOFF-2026-08-20.md` §2 for the arithmetic and the composition that shipped.
2. **The fixed floor** — 1,000 permanent seats that fill up. Built.
3. **How far to go now** — 1 to 10,000, one building. Campus and above are designed for and not
   built.
