The colony network, on the monitor

Past the planet the lens swings over the pole, and the picture becomes a strategy map. Star systems are joined by lanes, the studio's territory sits inside one bright border, and fog lies past the frontier. One glyph is one world of 100,000,000 desks. Zoom into any settled system and the lens flies down to that world, builds it, and keeps going to a desk with a person at it.

[ PLAY: ONE BILLION ] FROM THE GARAGE100M: LAUNCH JAMES10B: A HUNDRED WORLDS

Try: press AUTO on the map and watch the lanes fill. Tap any system for its card, then ZOOM IN. Drag pans the map. Scroll into a lit system to land on its world, then keep going to a desk. The title bar is an address: click NETWORK, EARTH, CITY, HQ or GARAGE to jump there.

Real stars, then catalogue stars

Proxima, Alpha Centauri, Barnard's Star, Sirius, Tau Ceti and about sixty more sit at their real distances from Sol. The map keeps distance and drops height. Past them are procedural catalogue stars (HD, GJ, HIP…). It's all a function of the seed, so nothing is stored.

The lanes decide the order

The next world is always the nearest one along the lanes, so the empire grows along its own supply lines. Proxima is still world 1, as §21.8 needs. Lanes never cross: a Gabriel graph, thinned to a strategy map's sparseness.

Territory and fog

Each settled system claims 3.4 light-years, and the lanes between them are claimed too. The border is the outline of the whole. Past the frontier the lanes run on as a faint web, so you can see there's more.

Hiring is the only verb

A world fills because the studio hires. There's no freight, no governor and no building queue. §7.7.1a's "not a colony manager" is kept: Stellaris is the look, not the game.

The title bar is the address

NETWORK > EARTH > CITY > HQ > GARAGE, with the current stop lit and every stop a jump. It's D's lift panel, moved into the one line a terminal would give it. On the map a legend and a light-year scale bar sit under it.

Pixel art, in the live hue

Every glyph is drawn at the monitor's own pixel and snapped to its grid. The whole map follows the entropy ramp: cyan with one world, red at a hundred. A drag pans instead of spinning, which fixes the galaxy's over-sensitive drag.

The backbone

What all four share

The options only disagree about how the far scales look. These parts are the same in every one, and they're what answers "zoom back in to every level each person is a person".

A seat is an address

Every developer has a place: world, tower, storey, desk. Who they are is the game's own developerAt(seed, i), so developer 7 in the garage is the same person here as in the game. Nothing is stored. The renderer draws whoever lives where you're looking, at any zoom, on any world.

One window is one developer

Each lit window is a specific seat, and a tower is exactly as tall as its filled storeys. The frontier tower is the half-lit one. Up close, towers are cut open below your eye like the garage's cutaway, and anything standing between the lens and the garage is drawn as an outline.

One lens, never a cut

Zoom goes where your pointer is. Drag moves over the ground, turns the globe from orbit and pans the map. Flights go up, across and down (Powers of Ten), even between worlds. The YOU and JAMES heads always fly you home (§7.7.4, the Hero Anchor).

Your garage, dropping storeys

Your idea, and already canon: §7.7.2 has "a complete, furnished, already-populated storey drops out of the sky". It also honours your 25 September line, "I don't want my garage moved to a building". The first storey drops across the lane behind the garage, not across the street in front, because the camera looks from the front and a tower there would hide the founder.

An abstract globe, full at 100M

Per your note: no real map. The planet is a ball of the city's own square blocks (a cube-sphere, so still no hex), 129,645 towers, holding exactly 100,000,000. It's toy scale on purpose. At true scale a hundred million people are a speck on Earth, and "Earth full" can't be a picture. Every colony is the same kind of ball with its own seed.

The satire is in the light

Output grows like √headcount (locked decision 1), and LOSS VIEW colours every window by what that person is doing: meetings, waiting, handoffs and so on. In the garage most people are working. At a hundred million, 0.07% are, and from orbit the planet is a patchwork of meetings (blue, the biggest slice), waiting and duplicate work (ambers) and handoffs (teal).

Side by side

What each one gets you, and what it costs

A · The Monitor

Gets you

  • It fixes the part that failed twice. The empire stops trying to be a place (the Pixi globe, the rebuild's city) and becomes the interface you already approved: STUDIO_OS and the isometric trees.
  • A clear rung where you asked for one: the world close up, the dashboard far out. The camera still never cuts.
  • The cheapest at 100M, and one hue at a time, driven by entropy (ART_DIRECTION §1.1).
  • The thesis without a caption: the bigger the studio, the more the founder sees people as a dashboard.
  • A colony map is already an interface, so the top of the ladder is where the monitor is strongest.

Risks

  • Reading as a chart. It holds only while a tap still names a person and a zoom still lands in a building, and in the demo both do on every world.
  • The entropy hue is red by the planet and stays red on the map. That's right by the rules, but is it the late-game mood you want?
  • Two looks to keep in step at the hand-off: the tower and its glyph must be the same size in the same place.

B · Little Planet

Gets you

  • "Colonise the globe" literally: you watch the city pave the planet and can spin it in your hand.
  • One representation all the way. There's no second look to maintain.
  • Physical and night-lit throughout, the approved garage mood.

Risks

  • It's the rebuild's complaint waiting to happen ("generic"). A planet of towers needs landmarks and authored districts to feel like yours.
  • Heaviest on a phone: 130K towers at once.
  • Fine grids shimmer from orbit. The demo softens it (towers sink into a street-light web), but it takes care.

C · The Swarm

Gets you

  • The most literal "swarm of devs": every dot is a person, and the colours are the loss partition at every scale.
  • The satire is always on screen, not behind a toggle.
  • Cheap: a point cloud, capped at 700K dots, with the scale bar saying when one dot stands for more than one person.

Risks

  • The most "data" of the four. It is people, but it looks like a visualisation.
  • Past 700K, one dot is several people. The count is honest, but the picture is sampled.

D · Dioramas

Gets you

  • Closest to GDD §7.4a: discrete stops, each an addressable unit. The lift panel is always the address.
  • Every rung is a composed shot you can art-direct and keep free of clipping (禁止穿模), with bounded performance.
  • Diving into any tower or district is explicit, not a zoom you have to aim.

Risks

  • Less infinite and less swarm. The mass is felt one rung at a time.
  • The rung edges are stops you notice, close to the "background shift" you rejected in the rebuild.

Honest

What these demos are not

  • They're demos, not the game. There's no economy beyond the headcount, and hiring is instant.
  • Tested at 60 fps on a desktop GPU (RTX 3060) and in a phone-sized frame. Not yet on a real phone, where B's 130K towers is the likely problem.
  • The James launch is a camera path with captions. The directed nine-shot scene in §21.8 is still to do, as you said it could wait.
  • A colony world is built in 3D when you first look at it, one at a time besides Proxima b. Leaving one for another lets the first go.
  • The map is a drawing: distance from Sol is real, height isn't, and one light-year is 20 km of the toy planet's space.
  • The border is circles and lane bands, not a smoothed outline, and labels drop out where they would overlap.
  • The cutaway and the x-ray are heuristics. They've been looked at, but not checked seat by seat for clipping.
  • In D, diving works from the district and city rungs. The garage rung only exists at HQ.
  • No sound yet.
  • Billy, Serena and Matt have no spaces in the garage yet. The build plan's phases 2 and 4 draw and then build them.
