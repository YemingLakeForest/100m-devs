# Handoff — 2026-08-20

The lens rebuild of `PLAN-2026-08-19-lens.md`, built. One camera, four levels, a fixed floor,
and a building you pick floors out of. Scope as agreed: **1 to 10,000 developers, one building**.

Read `PLAN-2026-08-19-lens.md` first — it holds the measurements that motivate all of this and
is not repeated here. This file records what was built, the eleven things that were found while
building it, and what is left.

## 2. Finding — ten un-occluded plates do not fit a 2.23:1 frame

The building was specified as an exploded plate stack: ten copies of the 5×2 floor at 1/10
size, fanned so each is separately tappable. **It was built twice and read wrong both times** —
first tiled (dx 178, gap 89), then overshooting (dx 205, gap 102, taller slab, shadow). Both
read as one continuous diagonal ramp rather than as ten floors.

The arithmetic says why, and it is not a tuning problem. A 2:1 plate of width `W` occludes the
one behind it unless the offset clears the diamond containment rule `dx + 2·dy ≥ W`. Stacking
ten of those inside a 997×448 frame caps `W` at about **145 px** — the plates end up smaller
than the tower bands they were supposed to replace. Arranging them as a column gets 84 px; as a
5×2 sheet, 187 px, which is a contact sheet and not a building.

**What shipped instead: a tower plus one pulled-out plan.** The tower is ~117×360 with 36 px
storey bands whose windows encode occupancy, and the storey you have open is drawn once, large,
beside it (`PLAN_X = 346`, `PLAN_Y = -309`), joined by a lift line. Tapping a band names a
storey; tapping it again enters. The plan slot does not move when the storey changes, so
picking your way up a building slides the contents rather than lurching the frame ten times —
pinned by `lens.test.ts` "changing storey does not move the frame".

`frames.ts`'s `TOWER_W`, `BAND_H`, `PLAN_X`, `PLAN_Y`, `PLOT_DROP` and `PLOT_SPREAD` are the
whole composition, and `plateAt()` keeps the signature the stack had, so a future stack is a
one-file change.

## 3. Finding — the numeral layer thinned by headcount, and headcount is not the problem

§8.2b thins the `+1`s by how many people are *drawn*. That was complete while a floor was the
only thing ever drawn. It stopped being complete the moment the lens could stand outside the
floor: a thousand seats are a thousand seats whether they measure 2,657 px across or 334, so the
hundred numerals that spread comfortably over a squad were stacked eight deep over a floor plan
the size of a postcard.

Two things were added to `tallies.ts`, both pure and both tested:

- **`SOURCE_CAP` / `capForLevel`** — how many numerals may fly, by level: 100 at Desk and Squad,
  **10 at Floor** (one per squad), **1 at Building** (one per storey).
- **`SOURCE_SPAN` / `spanForLevel` / `sourceWindow`** — *which* seats emit. The cap alone gets
  the count right and the placement wrong: a hundred sources over a thousand seats means one
  numeral per row of ten, and a row of ten is 2,700 px wide at Desk zoom, so the numeral lands
  on the mean of a row whose ends are both off screen. The layer now covers **the smallest group
  containing the address that it can draw a member of** — the address's own squad at Desk and
  Squad, the whole floor at Floor and Building. Squad-aligned, not centred on the seat: a window
  that slides with the camera pops numerals in and out at an edge nobody can see.

The result at Squad is the picture §8.2b actually asks for and had never once produced above a
hundred developers: a hundred people each emitting `+1` while the total barely moves.

## 4. Finding — a bubble nobody can read is not quiet dialogue, it is damage

§6.3 says dialogue is the product, and the ambient chatter counter-scales to stay legible — but
the counter-scale is clamped at 2.4×, so below a room scale of about 0.26 the words shrink
anyway. At Floor that put a dozen five-pixel white smudges over the plan looking like dropped
pixels. `ambient.ts` now has `BUBBLE_MIN_SCALE = 0.62` and skips drawing below it. The
behaviours keep running underneath, so the walkers still walk and the exchange you zoom back
into is the one that was already happening.

The test is the size the words land at — room scale times counter-scale — so it needs no level,
no camera and no threshold to keep in step with the ladder. It works out at: legible from Squad
inward, absent from Floor outward.

## 5. Finding — the camera rested where nobody lives, and the walk found it

`test:walk` went **bankrupt at item 9** on the first run of this rebuild: `devs: 1107`,
`cash: -1,000,133`, reproducible to the dollar. A clean worktree at HEAD passed the same item,
so it was this work.

The mechanism is worth writing down because none of it is about money. The lens opened, and
rested, at **Building** — and Building is the one level of the four with nobody on it. Past a
second storey the tower is tall enough that the pulled-out plan drops under
`ROOM_RESOLVE_SCALE` and the room is not drawn at all (`roomIsUp: false` at 1,107 developers on
a 640x360 frame, measured). Item 9 has to open the founder's screen, §13.6.7 says that door has
to be a person, so the walk does what a player would have to do: sweep the frame in 20x28 px
steps looking for its own avatar. Every one of those taps landed on a storey band of an empty
tower, all five hundred of them, while payroll ran at a thousand developers.

Two fixes, and both are products rather than gate repairs:

1. **The camera opens on the floor.** `Lens`'s constructor reframes to `FLOOR`, not `BUILDING`.
   The building is a place you *go* — it is how you choose which thousand people to stand among
   — and an idle game about poking developers should not open on an empty tower. §7.7.1's
   ceiling still owns the other end, so a one-developer garage opens on its desk exactly as
   before; this only decides where a studio big enough to have a choice comes to rest.
2. **The founder's hit box is a thumb at every zoom.** `founderHit`'s reach was
   `10 / floorScale + 4` local units, which works out at a *constant ten screen pixels* — a
   21 px target for §4.5d's "tap yourself", on a floor of a thousand people, at every level of
   the ladder. `FOUNDER_TOUCH_PX = 22` makes it a floor rather than a replacement: half of
   §23.4.2's 44 px box, contributing nothing at Desk zoom where the workstation is already
   hundreds of pixels across.

The second one is the more interesting defect. It was there before this rebuild and nothing
could see it, because the camera had never been anywhere the founder's desk was small.

## 6. Finding — the windows raked the wrong way, and so did the taps

Reported by eye: *"the tower windows do not look good, windows do not match."* Correct, and the
cause was one divisor. Every window was offset down its facade by
`(x + halfW) / TOWER_W * halfD` — **half** the slope the facade actually has, because the
divisor was the building's full width where the face is only half of it. So each row of glass
sagged away from its own floor, and a stack of them read as a shallow V fighting an inverted V
of floor seams. No amount of retinting would have helped. Two more of the same family: the
panes were `g.rect`, which on a raked wall is a sticker rather than glazing, and the roof
diamond was placed `HALF_D` low, so the top storey's faces stood proud of their own roof and
the crown appeared to be broken open.

`towerSurfaceY(x, level)` now lives in `frames.ts` and is the only place the rake is written
down. Everything is a point on it: faces, mullions, panes, floor plates, parapet, plinth — and
**taps**, which is where it stopped being a drawing problem.

`storeyAtBuilding` walked `bandRect` down the tower, and `bandRect` is an upright rectangle
while a storey is painted as a band sheared by `TOWER_D / 2` — 37.5 units against a 46-unit
storey, or **four fifths of a floor**. Pressing the middle of a storey you could see selected
the one below it, and pressing the lower half of the shaft selected nothing at all. §26.2.2's
whole claim is that a tap lands on the thing that was pointed at. Undoing the shear first turns
the test back into one division. Swept in a browser afterwards: a vertical line down the middle
of a ten-storey tower now reads 9,8,7,…,0 in order, and a horizontal line reads one storey
higher at the outer corners than at the near one, which is the rake being right.

Worth noting that a pre-existing test asserted the bug — it pressed `bandRect(f)`'s own centre
and got `f` back, because the code and the test were built from the same wrong rectangle. It
now aims at `towerSurfaceY`, which is what draws the thing.

### What the tower is now

A curtain-walled office slab at night, procedural, from the master palette (ART_DIRECTION §4
classes everything at this scale as T0/T3; no bespoke sprite).

- **Five bays per face, ten per storey — one per squad**, so the lit windows are not a bar
  chart drawn as windows. The left face is the floor's front row of squads and the right face
  its back row, and `squadsOnStorey` drives it directly instead of rounding a ratio back into a
  window count.
- **Monitor light, not lamp light.** Lit panes are the `GLOW` ramp; six per cent go amber on
  `WARN` and a fifth sit a step back, hashed from storey and squad so they never flicker
  between redraws. A floor where every window is the same cyan is a progress bar stood on end.
- **The mullion grid is drawn by not covering it**: the glazing band is laid down whole in the
  frame colour and the panes go on top, so the gaps *are* the mullions. Lighter than the
  spandrel, because mullions are extruded aluminium and are the one part of a curtain wall that
  catches the sky.
- **A floor plate** shows as a bright line once per storey, which is what makes ten floors
  countable at a glance, and a **parapet, plant room, lift overrun and mast** end it. The red
  aviation light needed `TOWER_CROWN` — the frame reserving headroom — because the first pass
  drew a mast and then cropped it off with its own viewport.
- **A plinth** with a lit lobby ribbon, because a lobby is lit whatever the hour and it is the
  only part of the building that says so when the studio is one person.
- Two rules learned the hard way and written into the colour table: never `NEUTRAL[0]` for a
  pane (that entry *is* the app background, so an empty storey came out as a black slot cut
  through the building), and every face of a roof box lighter than the deck (at `NEUTRAL[1]`
  against a `NEUTRAL[3]` deck they read as two pits punched in the roof).

The pulled-out plan got the same treatment for the same reason: filled flat in `WOOD[2]` it was
a correct occupancy reading and an orange rug, and above about seven storeys the room is below
`ROOM_RESOLVE_SCALE` and never draws, so that abstraction is the *only* picture of the floor.
Six banks of desks with a line of monitor light down each is the same information and is
legibly furniture.

### And the floor is inside the building you were just looking at

The tower is a glass slab whose windows carry the only information on it. Descend one level and
the same wall had **no windows at all** — two flat grey planes with a skirting and a cornice. The
floor did not read as being *in* anything, and two levels of one continuous zoom disagreed about
what the building was made of. `room.ts`'s office shell now carries the same construction seen
from the other side: precast spandrel to the sill, a glazing ribbon to the head, a transom rail
across it, and mullions drawn the tower's way — frame laid down whole, panes on top, so the gaps
between them *are* the grid.

Three things worth keeping:

- **The glass is `GLOW[0]`, not `NEUTRAL[1]`.** Painted in the darkest neutral it was not dark
  glass, it was an absence: the wall read as a colonnade of piers with the night showing between
  them, which is a car park. Glass at night is a very dark blue, and the palette has exactly one
  — the bottom of the ramp the monitors and the tower's lit windows already come from, so the
  thing you look *through* belongs to the same family as the thing you were looking *at*. A
  reflection band across the top of each pane is what finishes the turn from rectangle to glass.
- **What is out there is other towers.** Two or three lights a pane, hashed so the city does not
  rearrange itself on every hire. They are the exact thing the player was looking at one level
  up, at the size that level renders them — §7.2's continuous zoom told without a word.
- **Every rail is a fraction of the wall, never a pixel count.** The first pass inset the panes
  by "2" and made the transom "2" tall — two *room* units, which at Floor zoom is four tenths of
  a screen pixel, so neither existed. A wall forty pixels tall needs its rails measured against
  the wall.
- The window module is derived per wall from that wall's own run. The floor is five squads across
  and two deep and the two axes are not pitched alike, so a bay count that suits the long wall
  leaves the short one with panes half again as wide. A facade has one module.

Garages keep their brick and their two UPVC windows — the whole block is gated on `unfolded`.

## 7. Finding — nearest is the wrong rule for a magnetic stop

Reported as *"the zoom in got reversed forcibly back because we have not zoomed down enough…
this makes the UI very clunky"*, and that is an exact description of the mechanism.

`settleLevel` was `Math.round`. So a pinch had to travel **more than half a level in log space**
— a factor of about 1.8 — before it settled anywhere new; anything less and the magnetic stop
hauled the camera back to where it started. A deliberate, clearly visible zoom got silently
undone, which is the worst thing an interface can do to a gesture.

`settleTowards(level, from)` replaces it, and the rule is **intent, not proximity**: past
`SETTLE_INTENT` (a fifth of a level) a gesture always arrives *at least one level in the
direction it was going*, and nearest only breaks the tie when it has gone further than that. A
nudge leaves you where you were, a push moves you one, a shove moves you as many as it earned.
Levels count outward, so zooming in is a decrease and the sign falls out of the arithmetic.

Two details that are load-bearing rather than tidy:

- **The origin is held for the whole gesture**, not sampled at the end. The last frame of a
  pinch that overshoots and comes back points the wrong way.
- **A gesture that interrupts a flight is measured from where the camera *is*, not from
  `parked`.** `parked` during a commanded flight is the *destination*, so measuring against it
  scores a pinch on a level the camera has not reached — fingers moving in, arithmetic reading
  out, and the camera arriving one level further out than the gesture asked for. Pinned by
  "measures a pinch that interrupts a flight from where the camera is".

### And the selection now says so

*"The one I clicked is not the one I selected."* Measured: since §6's shear fix the mapping is
exact — a click at the painted centre of storeys 8, 5 and 2, on both faces and at the near
corner, selects 8, 5 and 2. So the defect that remained was **feedback**, not correctness: a
2 px cyan outline on a facade already made of bright horizontal rules is one more bright
horizontal rule.

The focused storey now says it three ways — a phosphor wash over its glass, a heavier outline,
and a solid tab hanging off the side of the band, outside the building's silhouette where
nothing else in the picture lives. In the lift panel the row gets a ground of its own and the
lift's own indicator tick. A *solid* phosphor slab was the first attempt at the row and was
worse: §3 draws this interface in 1 px rules and brackets and never in filled blocks, so the row
stopped reading as part of the panel at all.

## 8. Finding — you cannot tap a person from outside the building

`test:walk` failed item 9 with *"no drag and no tap reached the founder"*, and it reproduces
exactly: park the lens at Building with two storeys built and every tap in the walk's sweep is a
floor *selection*, so no number of them ever reaches a person. Five hundred taps found nothing,
and on the way they entered a floor by accident and descended — correct behaviour making a stale
test look like a broken game.

The leg was written on a premise the rebuild ended: *"poking yourself parks the lens on your own
desk and it stays parked."* It does not; the camera rests on the floor now, and past a thousand
developers the studio has a second storey it can be standing outside of.

A second run then failed the same leg from a different place: **lens at Squad, address on seat
400**, four squads from the corner workstation, where the sweep's three vertical drags were
never going to bring it back. Same root cause — with one camera the address can be anywhere, and
the leg assumed it was home.

So the walk stands on a floor before looking for a person on it, using §10's address ladder:
the **floor list** when the lens is outside the building, the **FLOOR rung** when it is
somewhere inside. Both are controls a player has, neither is a debug hook, and either lands the
lens on the floor with the corner desk in frame. On a run where the lens is already there both
are skipped and the sweep runs exactly as it always did. Verified from all three wrong places —
Building at two and at ten storeys, and Squad — each homes to Floor and finds the founder.

§4.5d's own answer would have been better and is not available: the rail's CODE button codes at
your desk *and* flies the lens there, but `.founder__corner` is `display: none` below 470 px of
frame by a decision §13.7.1a records and explicitly leaves to a human — and the walk, and every
screenshot in this file, run at 448. **So the game's one permanent "you are always present"
affordance is absent on the reference frame.** That is worth a decision rather than a patch, and
it is in §12.

That got the leg onto a floor and it *still* failed, from a third place: **at Desk on seat 494**,
having driven itself there. Which is the real finding, and it is about the sweep rather than
about the camera. The hunt taps a 20x28 grid over the whole frame, and that was fine while a tap
did nothing but select — but a tap navigates now, so five hundred of them walk the lens down the
ladder and away from the thing being hunted. "No tap reached the founder" had come to mean "the
sweep drove the lens to Desk on somebody else's squad".

So it aims instead. `__founderAt()` gives §7.8.10's corner in screen pixels — the same sanctioned
kind of hook as `__pick`, and there for the same reason: a gesture that missed looks exactly
like one that landed somewhere boring. One tap, and the leg keeps everything it proves — the
door is a person, and tapping that person opens the screen. It also splits a failure the sweep
conflated, which is the part worth having:

- *the avatar is not in the frame* — §13.7.1a's known trade, so the walk drags and asks again;
- *the avatar is in the frame at x,y and tapping it did nothing* — a bug, and the walk stops and
  says where it tapped.

Verified from every starting level at 40, 1,107 and 9,200 developers: one aimed tap opens it.
The passing run reports `it took 1 drag(s) on the floor to bring the corner desk back
(§13.7.1a)` — which is the leg doing exactly what it was written to observe, and now saying so
about a real drag rather than about four hundred stray taps.

Two other things came out of chasing it, both kept:

- `where()` now reports the **lens** as well as the store. A leg that fails on a tap has two
  suspects and the failure text only ever named one of them; "no tap reached the founder" with
  the lens outside the building is a different finding from the same words with the lens on the
  floor, and the walk could not tell them apart.
- The sweep checks **once a row, not once a tap**. Every `panel.count()` and `refuseBankruptcy`
  is a round trip, and at three per tap a four-pass sweep is five hundred round trips — twenty
  seconds. Item 9 runs at the developer cap with §4.1's payroll draining about four hundred
  thousand a second, so a leg that hunts for twenty seconds is a leg racing the bankruptcy
  modal, and it lost about half the time. Same points, same order; it just stops asking after
  each one.

## 9. Finding — a garage crosses the ladder, and a ceiling enforced from outside it cannot converge

Reported as *"when there's only me on new game, when I zoomed out, I can't zoom back in?"*.

Measured in a browser at 997×448 on a fresh career, it was not sluggishness. **Thirty wheel
notches, in and out, moved the camera from scale 8.42048 to scale 8.42048.** Two mechanisms, and
neither locks anything on its own.

**The garage crosses the ladder.** `Lens.scales()` overrides the floor's fit with the room's own
rectangle, because §7.8.1's first frames are a garage and framing a fixed 5×2 floor there is the
defect this whole rebuild is about, arrived at from the other end. But the garage is *smaller
than a squad* for the first thirty developers. At one developer, on the reference frame:

| Level | Fit |
|---|---|
| desk | 51.75 |
| squad | 8.42 |
| **floor** | **9.86** |
| building | 1.11 |

The floor sat *inside* the squad that contains it. An out-of-order rung is not a rung of the
wrong size — it is a rung that **does not exist**: `levelAtScale` walks the bands in order, so
the band between squad and floor inverted, every scale in it fell through to a level a level and
a half away, and the reported level jumped 1.7 across a scale change of nothing.

`nestScales` is the fix and it is one pass: **no level may frame less than the level inside it.**
Two levels that then frame the same rectangle *are the same place*, which is true of a garage —
"your squad", "your floor" and "everybody" name one group of people — and `levelAtScale` reads a
shared scale as the **inner** of the two, because a camera sitting on it got there by pulling
back until something stopped it, and the thing that stopped it was the inner rung's own ceiling.

**The ceiling could not satisfy itself.** §7.7.1's ceiling was held from outside the lens, once a
frame:

```ts
const ceiling = maxZoomFor(state.devs)
if (camera.z > ceiling) camera.set(ceiling)
```

That is an opinion about the camera's Z, held by something that cannot see the ladder the Z is
derived from. With the rungs crossed, the scale it asserted **reported a level above the ceiling**
— so it fired again on the next frame, and every frame after, for as long as the game was open.
Each firing threw away the gesture, the settle, the pan and the parked level. Every wheel notch
was undone 16 ms later.

It is a bound on the lens now — `Lens.setCeiling(level)` — and it is one bound on one clamp: the
zoom stops there, the settle will not target past it, and a commanded flight is held to it. There
is nothing left to fight because there is no second opinion. It is rounded on the way in, because
a ceiling is a *rung* and rungs are whole.

Worth stating plainly: **the whole reachable ladder for a studio of one is now Desk ↔ Squad, and
Squad frames the garage** — 9.86 rather than 8.42, the room the player is sitting in rather than a
squad-sized emptiness with the room in the middle of it. That is what the floor-rectangle override
was added for and it had never once delivered it, because the crossing hid it.

## 10. Finding — the primary verb moved the camera, and the gate had learned to live with it

Reported as *"when there's only James and us, clicking one of us to code, it should not zoom to
the person"*. Two separate paths did it, and in a two-person studio they are the only two things
on the screen to click.

**A tap on the founder flew the camera to the founder.** `codeAtFounderDesk` called
`focusFounderCamera` unconditionally, and it has two callers with opposite needs. The rail's
CODE — YOU *should* take the camera: §7.7.4 promises the way back to your own desk is always one
control away and it has to work from a floor you are not on. A tap on the founder in the world
should not — the finger is already on them, so the flight can only take the picture away from
where the player was looking, and it takes the *person* with it: the lens walks the corner desk
toward the middle of the frame, so the next tap of a studio whose entire script is TAP TO CODE
lands on empty floor.

**Any two taps inside 300 ms descended a level** — and the descend consumes the tap, so the second
click did not code at all. §7.7.6b boots Act I with POKE latched over a script that reads TAP TO
CODE. Clicking *is* the game there, and a player clicking at any speed a clicker is played at
produces a double tap on every second click: half the taps went to navigation, and the navigation
flew the lens onto the person being coded at. `descendOnDoubleTap(level, devs)` now gates it on
there being more than one squad to choose between — the lift panel's own argument, *a lift with
one button is furniture*, applied to a gesture instead of a control. Nothing is lost: the address
ladder, the wheel, the pinch and §7.7.6b's INSPECT tap all still navigate, and above a hundred
developers the double tap is untouched.

**And `test:walk` had already found this and worked around it.** `pokeAt`'s note says so, in the
file: *"the first version tapped a fixed point fifty times and got seven — `codeAtFounderDesk`
calls `focusFounderCamera`, so the first tap moves the target"*. The walk taught itself to
re-find the founder after every miss and carried on. **A gate that accommodates a defect stops
being able to report it**, and this one hid a broken primary verb for as long as it took a player
to notice. Item 3 now holds it directly: ten taps on one spot, all ten code, and the lens does not
move.

## 11. Finding — four cameras that never landed where they were sent, and one bug holding the others down

Fixing §9 broke `test:walk` at item 3 with *"no point on the canvas codes"*, and the finding is the
interesting half: **the external ceiling clamp had been re-cutting the camera onto a stop every
frame, and that side effect was masking three other cameras that never landed on one.** A fourth
came out of the same thread and is the one a player actually reported.

`Lens.set(z)` is the continuous door — it parks at exactly the Z it is given and switches the
magnetic stop off while it is there (`idleSince = Infinity`). That is right for `?z` and for the
§23.3 bench, which drive a dolly by stepping Z and would be measuring a staircase otherwise. It is
wrong for a driver that *stops*, and two of them stop:

- **The title screen.** §10.9.1's drift and push-in run through the real camera, one `set` per
  frame, and the title then unmounts mid-push-in. Measured on a cold boot: **level 0.35, not
  settling**, and §7.8.10's corner desk 148 px above the top of the frame. Act I's whole script is
  TAP TO CODE and there was nobody on the screen to tap. It hands back with one `reframe(FLOOR)`
  now — **the frame the lens opens itself on**, clamped by §7.7.1's ceiling, so a garage lands on
  the whole garage and a tower on a floor of it.
- **§10.7a.1's scene camera** saved `camera.z` on the way into a conversation and restored it with
  `set` on the way out. A Z sampled while the camera happened to be moving parks the studio
  between two levels for good. It saves a *level* now and restores it with `flyTo`, which is a
  stop, an ease rather than a cut (§10.5), and subject to the ceiling like every other flight.

A third, in the same family: **`setViewport` did not mark the frames dirty.** Every level's scale
is a fit against the viewport, so a resize moves all four rungs and a parked camera is left holding
a number that used to mean one of them. `parked`'s own note named the case — *"the viewport
rotates"* — and the flag was never set there. A cold boot lays the canvas out twice.

And the fourth, which is the one that was reported: **a camera sent to a person was being
re-centred on a rectangle.** `flyTo(DESK, room.founderDeskAt())` aims at §7.8.10's corner desk, and
the re-derive above then re-centres a parked camera on `frameOf(level)` — for Desk, that is
**seat 0, the first developer's chair.** The founder sits outside the seat lattice twelve units
away, so every time the frames moved the aim was quietly thrown away. Measured on a two-person
studio: the player's own avatar 395 px above the top of the screen, and a tap on it doing nothing.
Reported as *"I can't even click any of James or Me when there's 2 of us"*.

`parked` now has a companion, `parkedAt` — the point the camera was sent to, in floor space, or
null when it was sent to a frame. A camera sent to a person stays on the person.

**All four are the same mistake in different clothes: a camera was told where to be by something
that did not own where levels are.** The ladder is one place now (`frames.ts`), the camera is one
place (`lens.ts`), and everything else asks.

## 12. The floor picker

`StageHandle.nav()` reports the address, the storey list and its occupancy; `enterFloor(n)` and
`goToLevel(l)` drive the camera. `Lift.tsx` samples `nav()` at 10 Hz — the camera is not in the
store and does not notify, same argument as `PerfOverlay`.

It renders the address as a **ladder down the right rail** rather than a line of slashes,
because four rungs at 11 px fit a 146 px rail and a breadcrumb does not, and because the shape
matches the thing it describes. Under it, when the building is the subject, one row per storey:
number, occupancy bar, headcount. Tapping enters.

Two containment rules, both learned from the gate rather than guessed:

- **It is pinned to `--hud-rail`.** The right rail is `align-items: flex-end`, so a block that
  does not claim the width shrink-wraps to its content — which for `2ch | 1fr | 4ch` resolves the
  bar in the middle to nothing and draws the occupancy as an eight-pixel square.
- **It does not exist under two storeys.** A lift with one button is furniture, and
  `BUILDING 1 / FLOOR 01 / SQUAD 1 / DESK 1` over a room of forty people names four things that
  are all the same thing. §21's whole script runs under a thousand developers, so the acts
  anybody has played are untouched — which is also what keeps `test:ui-frame` green at 640×360,
  where the fullest HUD in the game has no spare pixels to lend. Below **400 px** of frame the
  address ladder folds away too and the doors stay. 400 and not the 470 the rest of `app.css`
  uses for short frames: 448 is the reference height every screenshot is taken at, and the
  ladder is the only door to the picker, so a rule that folds it there folds it in the only
  frame anybody looks at.
- **The doors and the way back out are never both shown.** Ten storeys of list plus four rungs
  of address is 190 px into a 136 px rail. The first attempt let the list scroll, which quietly
  hid floor 10 — a picker that cannot show one of the things you are picking from. They divide
  by level instead: outside the building you are choosing a floor, and the rungs under BUILDING
  name where you were last rather than where you are; inside one you need the way out, and the
  list would be a menu covering the thing it is a menu for.

## 15. Traps 45-48 — for the register in `HANDOFF.md`

**45. A defect can be held down by another defect, and fixing the second one is what makes the
first visible.** §7.7.1's ceiling was enforced from outside the lens by re-cutting the camera
every frame. That was wrong for its own reasons (§9) — and while it was there it was also, as a
side effect, putting a parked camera back onto a stop several times a second. Three separate
things were relying on that without anybody knowing: the title screen's push-in, §10.7a.1's scene
restore, and `setViewport`'s missing dirty flag. Removing the clamp did not create them; it
stopped paying for them, and the walk failed within a minute. **When a fix breaks a gate, the
first question is whether the gate was passing for the right reason.**

**46. A gate that can work around a defect will, and then it stops being able to report it.**
This is trap 40 one turn further on. That trap reads *"a harness that aims at a fixed screen point
is aiming at a camera that has moved — every canvas sweep re-finds rather than remembers"*, which
is true, is good practice, and is **not the finding**. The finding was in its own second sentence:
the game flew the camera on a *code tap*, so the person being coded at walked out from under the
thumb. Fifty taps landing seven was the game telling the harness it was broken, and the harness
wrote a helper. It stayed that way until a player said *"clicking one of us to code, it should not
zoom to the person"*. **When you find yourself compensating for the thing you are testing, the
compensation is the bug report.**

**47. An ordered ladder is only ordered if something makes it so.** `frames.ts`'s four levels nest
by construction — a desk is in a squad, a squad is on a floor — and then `Lens.scales()`
substitutes the room's *live* rectangle into one of them, and §7.8.1's garage is smaller than a
squad for the first thirty developers. `levelAtScale` walks the bands in order, so an out-of-order
rung is not a rung of the wrong size, it is a rung that **does not exist**. The invariant now has
a function and a name (`nestScales`) instead of being a property of the construction that one
caller was quietly allowed to break.

**48. A camera sent to a *person* is not a camera sent to a *level*.** The lens re-derives a
parked camera whenever the frames move, and it re-derived from the level alone — so a level's
frame silently replaced whatever the camera had been aimed at. It survived for as long as it did
because in most of the game the two agree closely enough not to notice; §7.8.10's founder is the
case where they do not, because they sit **outside the seat lattice** and no level frames them at
all. The camera was sent to a person, the frames moved a fifth of a second later, and it came to
rest on the first developer's chair with the player's own avatar off the top of the screen.
**When something can be aimed, the aim is state, and it has to be held next to the thing it
modifies.**
