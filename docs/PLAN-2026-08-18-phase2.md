# Phase 2 plan — UI and compute scale-up — 2026-08-18

Owner section: **[`GDD.md` §26.2](../GDD.md)**. Phase 1 closed the same day
([`HANDOFF-2026-08-18.md`](HANDOFF-2026-08-18.md), §26.1.8b), so this is the first thing
written against the new phase and nothing in it has been built yet.

**It opens with a measurement rather than a plan, because §26.2.1 — "the problem, stated
honestly" — is no longer honest.** It was written on 2026-08-15 and four of its clauses have
since stopped being true. A plan built on it would have spent its first week deleting a
per-developer simulation that does not exist.

Ordered by dependency, not by §26.2's own numbering. Each stage ends green (`npm run check`)
and is independently shippable, per [`PLAN-2026-08-11.md`](PLAN-2026-08-11.md)'s convention.

## 2. Six findings, and what each does to §26.2

### 2.1 The simulation already stopped tracking individuals — before Phase 2 started

§26.2.1 says *"Individuals are simulated individually, drawn individually, and interrupted
individually."* Measured against the source:

| §26.2.1's claim | What is actually there |
|---|---|
| simulated individually | **False.** `GameState.devs` is a `number`. There is no per-developer array anywhere in `store.ts`; `tick()` integrates §4.1, payroll, the tail, defects, incidents and tickets over scalars |
| drawn individually | **True, but only up to `ROOM_DEV_CAP = 1000`**, and `room.ts`'s own note records 59 fps at a thousand real `Container`s — *"the particle path was bought with a cost that was never charged"* |
| interrupted individually | **False.** `state.dev` is one `DevStateMachine` for the studio, not one per head |
| the room holds a few hundred before §23.3 is gone | **False.** It holds a thousand at 59.9 fps, and the cost does not move between 10³ and 10⁸ because the cap is on what is *drawn* |

§7.8.7's identity and §4.9a's output are already pure functions of `(seed, index)`, generated
on demand and never stored — which is §26.2.2's rule, shipped, days before the phase that
asks for it.

> **So Phase 2's thesis is already half-true by construction, and the phase is a different
> shape from the one §26.2 describes.** What is missing is not the aggregation. It is the
> **resolve path** — the half of §26.2.2 that says *"on demand means procedurally generated at
> the moment of the click"*. Nothing generates the hundred people inside a unit, because
> nothing can name a unit's hundred people. That is stages 2.0 and 2.1 below, and it is most
> of Phase 2.

### 2.2 The room draws seats 0–999 of the studio, whatever the studio is

`room.ts:2174` — `n = min(ROOM_DEV_CAP, headcount)`, and every seat is `seatFor(i)` counting
from zero. At a hundred million developers the picture is the same first floor as at a
thousand: the same thousand people, in the same thousand seats, with the same faces.

This is why §26.2.5's lines 2 and 3 have no mechanism today. "Zoom from galaxy to a single
desk" has nowhere to land — a desk in the room is *seat i of the studio*, not *seat i of the
block you tapped* — so there is no sense in which zooming out and back in could return you to
a different person, or to the same one.

**The spine for the fix already exists and is one function.** `units.ts`'s
`unitSeats(rung, index, devs) → {from, to}` resolves *any* unit at *any* rung to a seat range,
and §4.5b's poke has used it since R15. What is missing is that the room cannot be told to
draw `[from, to)` instead of `[0, 1000)`.

### 2.3 `ROSTER_CAP` makes gate line 4 vacuous as written

`output.ts:130` — `ROSTER_CAP = 10_000`, and `outputShare` returns **exactly 1** for every
seat past it. The reasoning is sound and was true when it was written: *"past it individual
developers are not drawn, not pokeable and not shown a numeral, so their rolls have nobody to
be compared with."*

§26.2.5's line 4 is the requirement that stops that being true:

> *A block's stated output equals the sum of the hundred people it generates, to the last
> story point.*

Take a block past seat 10,000. It states 100. It generates a hundred people of share 1 each.
They sum to 100. **The line passes, and nothing has been built.** It also passes while all
hundred of those people are identical, which is the exact opposite of §26.2.3's *"zoom in and
it resolves"* and of §4.9a's whole thesis.

> This is trap 44 aimed at a gate line instead of at a loop: **a vacuous pass is worse than a
> failure, because it is a failure that recruits everything below it as evidence.** Line 4 has
> to be written against a block whose hundred people visibly differ, and §4.9a's pinned mean
> has to survive it. Stage 2.0 is that piece of arithmetic.

### 2.4 Rungs 7–9 are an abstract node graph, on purpose, and that is where the gate lives

At 10⁸ the ceiling rung is **7** and the view is `grid` — glowing nodes, links and orbital
rings, §7.4's Level 3 tier standing in. `city.ts`'s header says so in advance and gives the
reason: *"Rungs 7–9 are a different register rather than more architecture. Building them
badly to fill the table would be worse than the honest gap."*

That was the right call and it expires here. **§26.2.5's gate headcount sits in rung 7**, so
the phase whose gate is a hundred million developers cannot leave the picture of a hundred
million developers as a placeholder. §26.2.4 already lists it.

### 2.5 The arithmetic survives the gate headcount; the loop does not reach it

Two halves, pointing opposite ways.

**The arithmetic is fine.** At 10⁸ devs against a cap of 2×10⁸ the readout shows
`VELOCITY 96,969,697`. §4.1 is `η = 1/(1 + L^ρ)` with `ρ = 5` (`entropy.ts:18`), so at
`L = 0.5` exactly, `η = 1/1.03125 = 0.969697`, and `10⁸ × 0.969697 = 96,969,700`. **The
speedometer reads what §4.1 says it should, to seven figures, with no overflow and no
precision loss — §26.2.5 line 1's third clause, already passing.**

**The loop does not get there.** §14.8.11's measured table ends at run 8 with **327,342**
developers — rung 5. A hundred million is two and a half orders of magnitude further on, and
§13.5 says so on purpose: *"days to weeks of play, across many Layer 1 Paradigm Shifts."*
Today the only route to that state is `?devs=`, which App.tsx is careful to describe as
*"deliberately not routed through the economy — it sets the count and nothing else"*.

**So gate line 1 cannot be walked until somebody decides what "a save at 100,000,000
developers" means.** It says *save*, and `?devs=` is not one: it never touches §24, so it
proves nothing about the thing line 1 actually asks — whether a document carrying that state
survives a round trip. Recommendation in stage 2.0.

### 2.6 A harness note, for whoever writes the walk

`page.mouse.wheel` did not move the camera in three attempts. The cause was §18.0a's bait
scene re-arming in the synthetic state, with `ui-dialogue__scrim` over the whole canvas at
`pointer-events: auto` — §10.7a.3 doing exactly what it promises. Dispatching the wheel on
the canvas element directly worked, and the ladder then walked cleanly on the first try.

**This is trap 44 in a new costume**, and it will bite the §26.2.5 walk harder than it bit the
§26.1.8 one, because every line of §26.2.5 begins by moving the lens. The walk needs a
`zoomTo(rung)` that asserts the rung actually changed and **fails loudly** when it did not —
not a loop that wheels twenty times and then measures whatever it happens to be looking at.

## 3. Two vocabulary collisions to settle before building on them

Both are cheap to settle now and expensive to settle after the level-of-detail ladder is
written in terms of them.

### 3.1 "A floor" means 1,000 in one section and 10,000 in another

| Source | A floor is |
|---|---|
| §7.7.1's rung table — rung 3 covers 10³–10⁴, unit = *a floor* | **1,000** |
| `units.ts` `BAND_FLOOR[3]`, read off §7.7.1 by construction | **1,000** |
| `tower.ts` `DEVS_PER_STOREY`, *"one storey holds a floor's worth of people"* | **1,000** |
| §7.8.1a's structure, and `room.ts` `FLOOR_SIZE = SQUAD_SIZE × FLOOR_SQUADS` | **10,000** |
| **§26.2.2's own LOD table — "Floor of 10,000"** | **10,000** |

Both are canon, both are built, and they are a factor of ten apart. §26.2.2's table is written
in the second vocabulary while the function that would implement it (`unitSeats`) is written
in the first.

### 3.2 §26.2.2's ladder and §7.7.1's ladder are different ladders

§26.2.2's LOD rungs are *desk / row / **block of 100** / **floor of 10,000** / building* —
which is §7.8.1a's **internal structure of the room**. §7.7.1's rungs are *person / floor /
building / campus / town / nation / planet / galaxy* — which is what the **camera** stops at,
and what `units.ts` implements. There is no rung in `units.ts` whose unit is 100.

**Recommendation, and it is a footnote rather than a redesign:** state in §26.2.2 that the LOD
ladder is §7.8.1a's (squad of 100 → floor of 10,000 → the §7.7.1 rungs above it), mark its
"floor of 10,000" as **§7.8.1a's floor, not §7.7.1's**, and leave both vocabularies standing.
Renaming either one touches four built modules and a dozen sections to buy what a footnote
buys.

## 4. The plan

### Stage 2.0 — The arithmetic and the fixture, pure

Nothing here touches the renderer, the store or the clock. This is the layer everything below
is built on, and finding 2.3 is why it is first.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `src/sim/aggregate.ts` *(new)* | **Hierarchical normalisation.** A unit's weight is a deterministic function of `(seed, rung, index)`, and a parent's weight is divided among its children **normalised within the parent**. Then `output(unit) ≡ Σ output(children)` **exactly, by construction, at every level**, and opening one unit costs O(branching factor) — never O(headcount). No table larger than a hundred, at any headcount |
| `src/sim/output.ts` | `ROSTER_CAP`'s flat tail retired *wherever the resolve path can reach*. Shares past 10,000 come from the block-local normaliser above rather than from the global prefix table, so a hundred people in a block at seat 50,000,000 differ from each other exactly as much as the first hundred do — with §4.9a's mean pinned **per block** rather than once globally, which is what makes the identity exact rather than statistical |
| `src/sim/units.ts` | Reconcile with §3.1's decision. `unitSeats` unchanged in shape — it is already right |
| `src/game/pacing.test.ts` | Extended past run 8 **until headcount crosses 10⁸**, emitting that run's save document as a fixture. This is the honest answer to finding 2.5: it *proves* the loop reaches the gate and *produces* the thing the gate loads, instead of asserting either |
| `scripts/scale.probe.mjs` *(new)* | §1's probe, promoted out of scratch. Not in `check` — it is an instrument, like `?bench`, so the next person can re-measure in one command rather than rebuild it |
| tests | The identity, at every rung and at seat ranges past 10⁸: `output(unit) == Σ output(children)` to the last story point; §4.9a's mean still pinned; the same `(seed, index)` still yields the same person |

**Rule to enforce in review:** no aggregate may be computed by summing a loop whose length is
a headcount. If a function needs `devs` iterations, it is the wrong function.

### Stage 2.1 — The resolve path: a unit you can open

The stage that makes §26.2.5 lines 2 and 3 mean anything.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `src/render/room.ts` | The room draws **a seat range**, not seats 0–999. `ROOM_DEV_CAP` becomes the size of the window rather than its position |
| `src/render/stage.ts` | Descending a rung (`descendOneRung`, already built) carries the **tapped unit's seat range** down with it, so the room you arrive in is the room you pointed at. Ascending discards it |
| `src/sim/identity.ts` | Unchanged — it already answers `(seed, index)` for any index. This is the payoff for it having been written that way |
| tests | A join test in the §26.1.8a idiom: descend into unit *k*, and the seats the room draws are exactly `unitSeats(rung, k, devs)`. **Nothing in it may supply its own left-hand side** |

### Stage 2.2 — The register changes with the rung

§26.2.3's first two bullets. Its third is stage 2.0's identity, already done by then.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `src/render/tallies.ts` | `groupSizeFor` already thins `+1` to `+56` **inside the room**. Extend the same rule above it: a storey, a building, a town emits one numeral for what it did |
| `src/game/chatter.ts`, `src/sim/ambient.ts` | §19 gets a register per rung. *"A floor does not say 'I can't push my code'; a floor says something a floor would say"* |
| `src/sim/poke.ts` | Verify §4.8's payout at every rung — §26.2.5 line 5. `units.ts` and `buffs.ts` already carry the seat-range machinery, so this may be a check rather than a build |

### Stage 2.3 — Rungs 7, 8 and 9 get a picture

### Stage 2.4 — What §26.1.6 deferred to here

| | |
|---|---|

### Stage 2.5 — §26.2.5, rewritten as things a player does, and walked

Per §26.1.8b: *"§26.2's thesis is a claim about what a player can reach and see, and therefore
a claim only `playthrough.acceptance.mjs` can be asked to check."*

Draft, for the GDD:

1. **Load the fixture save from stage 2.0.** The HUD reads a hundred million developers, the
   speedometer reads what §4.1 says at that headcount and cap, and the frame holds §23.3's
   budget. *(Three assertions, not one — and the third is the only one §1's probe could not
   make on a phone.)*
2. **Wheel from the ceiling rung to a single desk without a stall**, and the person at that
   desk has a name, a face and a history. *Every step asserts the rung actually changed.*
3. **Wheel back out and in again to the same unit: the same person.**
4. **Open a block anywhere in the studio — including past seat 10,000,000 — and its hundred
   people differ from each other**, and their outputs sum to what the block was stating before
   it was opened, to the last story point.
5. **Poke at every rung, and each pays what §4.8 says.**

Line 4 is finding 2.3's fix: it now names *where* the block is and *that its people differ*,
so it cannot be passed by a flat tail.
