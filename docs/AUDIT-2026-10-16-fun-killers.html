Fun killers

Everything in the current loop that doesn't work, isn't fun, is clunky, or kills the fun. The loop under review: the founder releases games to earn money and hires devs with it; Serena automates release and runs the queue; Billy is "more people, less efficiency"; Matt handles user requests, incidents and bad reviews (refunds stop a game earning); James is a super dev who codes super fast.

How this was measured

I read the core loop code (game/store.ts tick, ship queue, release ring, sim/revenue.ts, incidents.ts, support.ts, economy.ts, entropy.ts, heroRoster.ts, upgradeTrees.ts), then ran a throwaway vitest against the real store. A scripted player went through Run 1 to bankruptcy, took the shift, and played an hour of Run 2, logging every simulated minute. It pokes 3 times a second and presses SHIP! 8 seconds after a build lands. There were three variants:

  • careful: hires up to 76% of capacity (the game's own best-output point) and buys the cheapest working upgrade it can afford.
  • greedy: hires whenever it keeps 30 seconds of payroll in hand.
  • manual-only: never buys an upgrade.

The scripted player flatters the game: it never misses a ship and never misreads the speedometer. Treat every timing below as the best case. A headless-browser pass was cut short when the page reloaded during the pull, so this audit rests mostly on the numbers, not on hand play.

Findings, worst first

01Hiring never makes games ship faster

On the last project tier, project size grows with the studio's speed, aiming at TARGET_BUILD_SECONDS = 60 (economy.ts, commitmentFor). Measured build time was about 66s at 84 devs, 66s at 115 devs, and about 95s at 219 devs.

"Hire devs → ship faster" is the founder's whole promise, and it is flat: you get bigger numbers at the same rhythm. Only overhiring changes the pace, and it makes it slower. The studio never visibly speeds up, so the 1-to-100M fantasy has nothing to show it with.

touches GDD §4.10c / terminal rung sizing

02After about minute 10 of Run 2, money stops meaning anything

  • Manual-only: plateaus at 84 devs with $159M unspent after an hour; the next hire costs $9.5K.
  • Careful: ends on $83M. What limits it is capacity, and the only working capacity upgrades are Billy's, priced at "3 minutes of income" (pipelinePriceFloor). So the loop becomes: wait about 3 minutes, buy one node, hire about 5 people.
  • Greedy: the reverse. The next hire is $2M against about $20K/s income (30 · 1.08^n, cap-normalised). An exponential price wall stops it before the entropy lesson really lands.

There is no middle where money is a choice.

04The cast arrives all at once, then nothing happens for 50 minutes

Founder board at 5.3, Billy at 6.3, Serena at 7.8, James promoted at 7.9, Matt at 8.8. From minute 9 to 60, nothing new happens. Every system arrives in a 3.5-minute burst, and no problem gets time to hurt before its answer walks in. Now that THE THREAD is gone (56c56c2), there are no events at all to break up the hour.

05Matt's problems are paper tigers

  • Incidents: never more than 2 open in any run. When an incident hits, the game's tail pays out later instead of being lost, and incidents.ts itself says the cash curve "barely moves". The page also lands on the oldest game still on sale (candidates.find takes the first). That game earns almost nothing, so freezing it costs almost nothing.
  • Tickets: can take at most 20% off catalogue income (MIN_CATALOGUE_MULTIPLIER = 0.8). The queue climbed to 105–144, then went to 0 within about a minute of Matt arriving (HELPDESK_SHARE = 0.05 of headcount) and stayed there.
  • Bad reviews: only affect the payout multiplier and reputation. They show up as a review reel, not as anything that bites.
  • None of this gives the player anything to do. It's pressure you watch, and Matt switches it off permanently.

06Serena automates you out of the best-paid action

Auto-Ship starts at one release every 120s (AUTO_SHIP_BASE_SECONDS), which is slower than the roughly 66s builds, so you keep pressing SHIP! anyway. Auto-shipped releases count as an unattended launch (pipelineLaunch): ×1 on the date, readiness fixed at RC. A perfect manual ring pays up to ×1.75 plus a better rating. So the best play is to keep doing the ring by hand forever, roughly 60 times an hour, each one a modal that stops the studio. The 2026-10-16 ring rework makes each ring better, but not the 60th one in an hour. "Automate" ends up meaning you either accept less money or ignore the automation.

07Serena's queue-size upgrades are worthless if you're paying attention

A player who ships within 8 seconds was never blocked once in an hour, and the queue never held more than 2. The careful player still spent about 10 minutes of income on Shelf and Rack, ending with 25 slots it never used. "A full queue stops the floor" only punishes someone who's away, and the trees don't tell you that.

08Cash swings wildly every cycle

Most of a release's money arrives in the first ~10 seconds (SPIKE_TAU 4–8s, SPIKE_SHARE 50–68%), so income per second jumps around: 0 → 284K → 5K. In one run, cash went from +$146K to −$233K between two ships at 84 devs, about 3 minutes from bankruptcy, while the studio was actually healthy. revenue.ts says the payout-over-time model was written to stop the studio crossing zero every project cycle, and it still does.

09More people, less efficiency (Billy's lesson) just turns into a stall

Greedy player: entropy sits at about 0.80 for 50 minutes, devs creep from 148 to 219, and the cash balance hovers around $0.5–2M. There's no collapse and no bankruptcy in sight, so nothing clearly tells you that you messed up. Run 1's trap is the opposite: a scripted bankruptcy about 4 minutes in. One lesson is a cutscene, the other never comes back as a real consequence.

10The scale ladder doesn't move

After an hour of Run 2, capacity went from 105 to 151 and headcount topped out at 115–219. In a game called 100,000,000 Developers, the screen barely changes in the first hour after the tutorial. The garage-to-galaxy art has nothing to show.

11Run 1 is about 4 minutes, then forced bankruptcy

Mass Hire at 3.6 minutes, bankrupt at 4.0. That's three ships, one decision, and a loss you couldn't avoid. It's canon in the GDD (§21), but it's where a first-time player decides whether to keep going, and it's mostly cutscenes.

12James is just a click multiplier

His tap is 2× the founder's tap (JAMES_TAP_MULTIPLE), and he keeps typing through stand-ups. The gym joke is 10:00–11:00 of the studio clock (sim/james.ts), which means about 10 hours into a session, so nobody will ever see it. His tree has 3 working nodes. "Super dev who codes super fast" never shows up as speed, for the reason in finding 01.

13Smaller clunk

  • Clicking anything does nothing while the queue is full, apart from a nag bubble (nagFullShelf). It feels like the game has frozen.
  • At 12 devs the HUD was already showing defects, an incident, queue fill time, net burn, gridlock and an event card: a lot of readouts for decisions the player mostly can't make yet.

If only three get fixed

  1. Let hiring speed up the release rhythm (01). That brings back the core feeling.
  2. Give money a constant job (02, 03): wire the trees so cash always has somewhere to go, and make capacity something you can buy, not something you wait for.
  3. Give Matt's and Serena's systems a player verb, or let them actually cost something (05, 06). Make automation a real trade, not either a tax or something you ignore.

Several findings are written into the GDD: 01 (terminal rung sizing), 11 (§21's trap), the ring's ×1.75 and the 0.8 ticket floor. Changing any of them means amending the GDD in the same batch.
