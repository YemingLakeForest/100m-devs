# Handoff — 2026-08-23

One level, added on top of `HANDOFF-2026-08-22.md`'s six. **Seven levels**, scope **1 to
100,000,000 developers** — a hundred sites of a million, on a planet.

That handoff's §22 left *"above the park"* as the next thing and called it a town. It is a
**planet**, and §0 is the argument for that. It came to one more division, a new `TOP_LEVEL`,
two new files, and **no LOD threshold moved by a single digit** — the third time running, which
is what turns a claim into a property of the model.

Read `HANDOFF-2026-08-22.md` first, then
[`PLAN-2026-08-23-globe.md`](PLAN-2026-08-23-globe.md) for the arithmetic and the decisions
behind it. Demos: **https://claude.ai/code/artifact/1aa88f10-1166-4e2d-a100-11738e49a77f**.

**Four of the six findings below were invisible to the arithmetic and obvious in a picture**, and
every one of them had green tests underneath it at the time. That is the through-line of the
session and §7 is the tool built because of it.

## 0. The amendment — the planet is full at the number on the box

GDD §7.7.1 puts 10⁸ at rung 7, *a nation*, and a planet at 10¹⁰–10¹³. **This puts the full
planet at 10⁸**, so the number the game is named after and the moment the world runs out of
ground are the same event.

Deliberate, and not drift. §7.8.2 had already conceded that rung 6 spans two decades where every
rung below spans one, and therefore *"does not come for free the way rung 5 did"*. This spends
both decades at once and takes the game's title as the payoff; rungs 7–9 are freed to be what the
GDD always wanted them to be, which is off Earth entirely.

## 2. The arithmetic is derived, not tuned

`globeRadius()` has no free parameter in it. One requirement fixes it: **a hundred parks exactly
tile the sphere.** A site's share of a sphere of radius `R` is `4πR²/100`, so its width is
`sqrt(4π/100)·R`; setting that equal to the park's own width in globe space gives
`R = 1.410 × parkCell().w`, and `frames.test.ts` measures the coverage at **100.00%**.

Three things fall out and not one is a knob:

- Globe frame `2R` = **2.82 park widths**, so the step out is ×11.28.
- One park spans **20.3°** of the planet, so its sag against the tangent plane is **1.57% of its
  own width** — **3.3 px** at the fit. That number is the whole reason §3's hand-off is a swap.
- **The planet is full at exactly 100,000,000.**

### The step, and the number the plan got wrong

The plan said ×5.64 before it was measured. That was the *width* ratio, and the fit is governed by
whichever axis binds: **a circle needs as much height as width where a 2:1 park needs half**, so
framing the planet costs double what the width ratio suggests. The measured ×11.28 is not an
outlier and `frames.test.ts` asserts why: the five single-decade steps below have a geometric mean
of ×3.86, **two of them compound to ×14.9**, and rung 6 spans two decades. A two-decade rung
taking *less* than two rungs' worth of step is the opposite of a problem.

### §7.8.2 rule 1 is spent here, on purpose

*Every unit occupies the same footprint* is kept at every rung below and **spent** at this one. The
unit did not change from *park* to *bigger park*; it changed to *the planet*, and a planet drawn
one park wide is not a planet. Rule 2 is kept and does all the work instead — a sphere is not a
lattice, so the silhouette could hardly change more.

## 3. The hand-off — *"not a patched-on grey sticker"*

This is the part worth the ink, and it is the correction that reshaped the level. The first mock
drew a campus as a flat neutral slab laid on green ground and it read as exactly what it was.

> **Everything the studio *built* is neutral. Everything it *stands on* belongs to the planet.**

The board, its coast and its rim take the site's terrain ramp. The decks, packages, bus, plant,
pin rows and towers stay `RAMPS.NEUTRAL` — **concrete is grey wherever you pour it**, and a deck
that took the biome's colour would be a campus in camouflage. That split is why the change to
`park.ts` is one function and one redraw rather than a rewrite.

Three things have to agree for the swap to be invisible, and each is now true by construction:

1. **Same footprint.** A campus glyph is drawn from the park's *own* plot lattice through
   `latticeOnGlobe`, whose tangent basis is chosen so that at the sub-camera point it reduces
   **term for term** to `parkLatticeAt` — `((u−v)·sx, (u+v)·sy)`. `worldMap.test.ts` asserts the
   reduction and then asserts that the residual is the *gnomonic* correction `θ²/2` rather than
   any old small number, so nobody can quietly swap the projection for a different one.
2. **Same colour.** Both sides call `terrainShade(biome, step, −1)`.
3. **Same light.** Both read `globe.groundAt(site)`. **The sun lives on the planet**, because a
   park working out its own daylight would be a second sun and the first thing two suns do is
   disagree.

So the park is not composited onto the globe — it is the same picture at two levels of detail, and
the cross-fade between them is the honest kind. `globe.test.ts` gates both halves.

### And the planet does not spin

It is turned by the **address**: `orientationFor(site)` brings the site the camera is in to face
the camera, and nothing else moves it. A clock-driven spin would make a park's position inside the
globe a function of time, and `frames.ts` needs it to be a constant or rung 6 is not a place the
camera can be *sent* to. The sun moves instead.

## 4. Traps 64–68 — for the register in `HANDOFF.md`

**A note before the list.** Rung 6 also costs a *rebuild* rather than only a draw, and
`HANDOFF-2026-08-22.md`'s trap 62 — *a `Graphics` costs what is in it every frame* — applies one
level along. The planet is fourteen hundred polygons and the sun moves it every two and a half
seconds, so it rebuilds **only while it is visible** and starts hidden: a new game is one
developer in a garage, and without that gate it spends a full rebuild at boot and another every
two and a half seconds on a world nobody will see for an hour. `globe.test.ts` holds it.

**64. An enum's order is not an intention.** The site lattice was sorted `surfaceAt(b) −
surfaceAt(a)` to mean *land before sea*. `ICE` is 2 and `LANDMASS` is 1, so it meant **ice
first**: the studio's opening campus was in Antarctica, and because the planet turns to face site
0, every render of rung 6 opened on the south pole. Every number was correct. Sort by a rank you
wrote down for the purpose (`SETTLE_RANK`), never by an enum that happens to be ordinal.

**65. Nothing may be the colour of the background, least of all half a planet.** Four of the five
terrain ramps opened on `NEUTRAL[0]`, which is `background: 0x14121a`, which is space. The unlit
half of the world was exactly the colour of the gap around it, so a full sphere rendered as a dome
with a bite out of it. `worldMap.test.ts` now holds every ramp's first entry against `SKY`.

**66. A file that draws in world units may not be given a size in pixels.** The campus lights and
the address bracket were sized 11, 4 and 20 — plainly pixels, in a file where the planet is ten
thousand across and one unit is a twelfth of a pixel. All of them were sub-pixel; the planet had
no lights on it. Everything in `globe.ts` is a fraction of `R` now, and the header says why.

**67. Fading a container fades everything parented into it, including the thing you are standing
in.** `park.ts` lives *inside* `globe.ts` — that is the whole nesting — so
`setChromeAlpha` on the globe's root took the entire studio with it. At rung 1 the planet's alpha
is zero, so **a new game opened on a black screen with a HUD over it**. The planet's own layers
had to become a container of their own. Caught by `test:ui-frame`'s degenerate-pinch case, which
was written for an unrelated NaN bug two handoffs ago and has now caught something it was not
aimed at — which is the argument for a gate that asserts *the picture is not blank* rather than
one that asserts a particular cause of blankness.

**68. When a space moves, every conversion out of it moves.** `camera.toWorld` returns the
outermost space, and the outermost space became the globe. Two pickers fed its result straight to
park-space functions and were silently out by a factor of two — a *uniform scale about the
origin*, which still works near the middle of the screen and fails at the edges. `test:ui-frame`
reported it as **nine of ten buildings reachable**, which is a far better description of the
defect than the arithmetic would have managed. There is one `inPark()` now and every picker above
the room goes through it.

## 5. How to look at a planet before it is on screen

`src/render/__preview.test.ts` records the real `Graphics` calls `globe.ts` makes and writes them
out as an SVG at the reference frame, at the scale the lens settles on for rung 6:

```
PREVIEW_OUT=globe.svg PREVIEW_DEVS=4000000 PREVIEW_T=0.62 npx vitest run src/render/__preview.test.ts
```

It is skipped without `PREVIEW_OUT`, so it neither runs under `npm test` nor writes a file. **It
exists because traps 64, 65 and 66 were all invisible to the arithmetic**, and it found all three
in about ten minutes. It is the only file excluded from `tsconfig.app.json`, and the exclusion has
a comment on it: it needs `node:fs`, and widening the app's type surface to `@types/node` would
let shipped code reach for a Node API and still typecheck.

## 7. The headcount box — `?scenarios`

`applyScenario` was already a function of one number with a button on the front of it. The button
came off: `applyHeadcount(devs)` and `parseHeadcount(text)`, and the bar gained a box that takes
`250000`, `40m`, `1e8`, `0.5b` or `100,000,000` and puts the studio at exactly that, with the lens
parked at the rung the number has earned.

The decades on the buttons are a list of *interesting* sizes and the interesting sizes are not
always decades — rung 6 spans two of them, so *"what does forty million look like"* had no button
and *"what does the frame after the sixty-first site look like"* could only be asked by typing
61,000,001. There is a `SITES n/100` readout beside `DEVS`, `RUNG` and `VIEW`.

Two details that are not decoration: the box goes **red** rather than clamping, because a typo
that silently becomes a studio of nobody is worse than a border that stays red; and the `1`–`9`
scenario shortcuts are suppressed while an `<input>` has focus, because otherwise typing
`40000000` loads scenario 4 and then eight studios of one developer.

## 9. What was not touched

The whole of Run 1 is under a thousand developers and none of it can see rung 6, which is why the
walk passed unchanged and why this level could be built without opening `store.ts` at all. The
economy, the entropy engine, the prestige trees, the scene system and the hero board are all
exactly as `HANDOFF-2026-08-22.md` left them.

The one seam worth naming: **`park.ts`, `block.ts` and `building.ts` never learned that a planet
exists.** They already drew *a park given a headcount*, and what changed is that `stage.ts` hands
them `devsOnSite(devs, site)` instead of the studio's whole payroll — one argument at each of
**sixteen** call sites, and the compiler found every one because `site` was made a *required*
parameter rather than a defaulted one. That is trap 52a's lesson spent on purpose a rung later.
